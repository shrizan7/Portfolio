The study

Before number (baseline)

Measure one number the way things are today, before changing anything. Example: cables per shift, minutes per unit, or operators needed.

Demand and takt time

Takt time is how often one unit must be finished to meet demand: available time ÷ units needed.

—

Allowances (PF&D)

Extra time added on top of the measured work for personal needs, fatigue and small unavoidable delays. These defaults are common textbook values. Replace them with your plant’s rule if it has one.

—

Machine-paced steps get no rating and no allowance by default, because the machine sets their speed. You can change that per step on the Standard times tab.

Steps in one cycle (work elements)

Timing is running. Stop it to change the steps.

List the steps in the order the operator does them. Pick a clear start and end point for each step (for example “hand touches cable” to “cable lands in the cutter”). “Comes after” lists the step numbers that must be finished first. It is used for line balancing. Leave it empty if a step can happen any time.

Readings (seconds)

Each row is one cycle. You can type or fix any number here. A crossed-out number is left out of the results (flagged as an interruption). Turn it back on from the Standard times tab.

Standard time per step

Tap a reading to leave it out (or put it back). Orange readings are more than 30% away from that step’s middle value. Check whether something unusual happened. Rating: 100 = normal pace for a trained operator, 110 = 10% faster than normal, 90 = slower.

How the numbers are worked out
Average = mean of the readings you kept
Normal time = Average × Rating ÷ 100
Standard time = Normal time × (1 + Allowance ÷ 100)
Per unit = Standard time ÷ “happens once every N units”
Takt time = Available seconds per day ÷ Units needed per day
Readings needed (95% confidence, ±5%) = ( 40 × √(n·Σx² − (Σx)²) ÷ Σx )²
Minimum operators = Total per-unit standard time ÷ Takt time, rounded up

Auto-balance fills each station up to the target, starting with the steps that have the most work after them (the ranked positional weight method), and never puts a step before one it has to come after. Leave the target empty to use takt. A lower target leaves room for problems.

Send times to Layout Extruder

Puts your measured times on the stops of a Layout Extruder flow path, so its cycle and waiting times use real numbers.

  1. In Layout Extruder, press Save paths & text to file.
  2. Open that file here. Each stop gets matched to a station or step with the same name.
  3. Check the matches, then save the new file and load it back into Layout Extruder with Load paths & text from file.
